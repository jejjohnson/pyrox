Kernels & GPs · L2 column → inversion row

Is the averaging kernel a KISS-GP interpolation?

Half yes. Both are a linear map \(\mat{H}\) from a GP on a regular grid to the things you observe, so both give an observation covariance \(\mat{H} \mat{K} \mat{H}^\top\). But they act on different axes, they play different roles, and the averaging kernel has a second identity that KISS-GP's weights do not: it is the smoother matrix of the retrieval.

TL;DR

Same algebra, different job

  • The shared core is real. Any linear map of a Gaussian field is Gaussian, with covariance \(\mat{H} \mat{K} \mat{H}^\top\). KISS-GP's \(\mat{W} \mat{K}_{UU} \mat{W}^\top\) and a column operator's \(\tilde{\mat{A}} \mat{K}_z \tilde{\mat{A}}^\top\) are both instances.
  • KISS-GP's \(\mat{W}\) is horizontal and approximate. It interpolates a grid to a point: 16 nonzeros per row in 2-D, rows sum to 1, and the error vanishes as the grid gets finer. It is a computational trick.
  • The column averaging kernel \(\tilde{\vect{a}}_n\) is vertical and exact. It collapses the layer dimension to one XCH₄ number: dense over all layers, not summing to 1, and set by the instrument's physics. You cannot refine it away.
  • In a methane inversion they compose. One observation row is \(\tilde{\vect{a}}_n^\top \otimes \vect{w}_n^\top\). With a separable prior \(\mat{K}_z \otimes \mat{K}_s\), the column covariance is \((\tilde{\mat{A}} \mat{K}_z \tilde{\mat{A}}^\top) \circ (\mat{W} \mat{K}_s \mat{W}^\top)\).
  • What you were missing: the averaging kernel comes from the retrieval's posterior. With an identity Jacobian it is exactly the GP smoother matrix \(\mat{K}(\mat{K} + \sigma^2 \mat{I})^{-1}\), and its trace, the DOFS, is the GP's effective degrees of freedom. \(\mat{W}\) lives in the prior and has no such meaning.
  • Notation trap: in Rodgers' notation \(\mat{K}\) is the Jacobian, so "\(\mat{A} \mat{K} \mat{A}^\top\)" reads two ways. On this page \(\mat{J}\) is a Jacobian and every \(\mat{K}\) is a covariance.

1 · The shared core: looking at a GP through a linear map

The real problem: the model carries a 3-D field \(\vect{x}\) with \(L G\) numbers, and TROPOMI reports one XCH₄ number per footprint. Anything that connects them is an observation operator \(\mat{H}\). The question you are asking is about the shape of \(\mat{H}\), and the one fact that makes both KISS-GP and the averaging kernel tick is this.

Concept

Linear maps of a Gaussian stay Gaussian

\[ \vect{x} \sim \mathcal{N}(\gvect{\mu}_x, \mat{K}) \] \[ \vect{y} = \mat{H} \vect{x} \] \[ \vect{y} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mat{H} \gvect{\mu}_x, \mat{H} \mat{K} \mat{H}^\top) \]

Every row of \(\mat{H}\) is a linear functional: point evaluation, interpolation, a footprint average, a weighted vertical integral. The covariance between two observations is the kernel "integrated twice", once against each row.

Where \(\mat{H}\)observation operator\(\mathbb{R}^{N \times L G}\)— \(\mat{K}\)prior covariance of the state\(\mathbb{R}^{L G \times L G}\)ppb²

So your instinct is right at this level: \(\mat{W} \mat{K}_{UU} \mat{W}^\top\) and \(\tilde{\mat{A}} \mat{K}_z \tilde{\mat{A}}^\top\) are the same sandwich. What differs is which rows \(\mat{H}\) has and why.

Running example

\(L G\) is 12 layers times every grid cell in the domain; \(N = 3\) pixels. \(\mat{H}\) is \(3 \times 12 G\), and almost every entry is zero.

2 · KISS-GP: \(\mat{W}\) stands in for point evaluation

In ordinary GP regression the observation is \(f(\vect{s}_n)\), a point evaluation, and the Gram matrix \(\mat{K}_{XX}\) is dense, costing \(\mathcal{O}(N^3)\). KISS-GP (structured kernel interpolation) replaces each point evaluation by interpolation from a regular grid of inducing points \(U\).

Concept

Structured kernel interpolation

\[ k(\vect{s}_n, \vect{s}_m) \approx \vect{w}_n^\top \mat{K}_{UU} \vect{w}_m \] \[ \mat{K}_{XX} \approx \mat{W} \mat{K}_{UU} \mat{W}^\top \]

\(\vect{w}_n\) holds local cubic convolution weights \(c(\cdot)\): 4 per axis, so 16 nonzeros in 2-D, summing to 1. \(\mat{K}_{UU}\) on a regular grid with a stationary separable kernel is Kronecker of Toeplitz, so a matrix–vector product costs \(\mathcal{O}(N + G \log G)\).

Where \(\mat{K}_{UU}\)kernel on the grid; here \(\mat{K}_s\)\(\mathbb{R}^{G \times G}\)— \(c\)Keys cubic convolution kernel, support \([-2, 2]\)\(\mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}\)—

The point to hold on to: the "true" row of \(\mat{H}\) is a delta at \(\vect{s}_n\). \(\vect{w}_n\) is an approximation to that delta, chosen because it makes \(\mat{K}\) cheap. Refine the grid and \(\vect{w}_n^\top \mat{K}_{UU} \vect{w}_m \to k(\vect{s}_n, \vect{s}_m)\).

Running example

With \(\Delta s = 25\) km and \(\ell_s = 50\) km: exact correlation between the 7 km neighbours is 0.990; KISS-GP gives 0.984. For the 60 km pair, 0.487 against 0.487. The diagonal comes out at 0.997, not 1.

Watch out

Cubic weights can be negative and the interpolated diagonal sits slightly below the true variance. That small variance deflation is harmless for regression but matters if you read \(\op{diag}(\mat{W} \mat{K}_{UU} \mat{W}^\top)\) as a prior uncertainty; add the diagonal correction if you do.

3 · The column averaging kernel collapses height

Yes: the column averaging kernel collapses the layer dimension. The retrieved XCH₄ is not the true pressure-weighted column \(\vect{h}^\top \vect{x}_n\). It responds to each layer with sensitivity \(a_{n,l}\) and falls back to the retrieval's prior \(\vect{x}_{a,n}\) where it is blind. To compare a model with TROPOMI you apply the same filter to the model profile.

Concept

Column observation operator

\[ \tilde{\vect{a}}_n = \vect{h} \odot \vect{a}_n \] \[ y_n = \vect{h}^\top \vect{x}_{a,n} + \tilde{\vect{a}}_n^\top (\vect{x}_n - \vect{x}_{a,n}) \] \[ \op{Var}(y_n) = \tilde{\vect{a}}_n^\top \mat{K}_z \tilde{\vect{a}}_n \]

If \(\vect{a}_n = \vect{1}\), the retrieval sees every layer perfectly and \(y_n = \vect{h}^\top \vect{x}_n\). Deviations from 1 are the instrument: SWIR sensitivity drops near the surface over dark scenes and in the stratosphere.

Where \(\vect{h}\)dry-air pressure weights, \(\sum_l h_l = 1\)\(\mathbb{R}^{L}\)— \(\vect{a}_n\)column averaging kernel, reported per pixel\(\mathbb{R}^{L}\)— \(\vect{x}_{a,n}\)prior profile used by the retrieval\(\mathbb{R}^{L}\)ppb

Compare it with \(\vect{w}_n\). \(\tilde{\vect{a}}_n\) is dense over all 12 layers, its entries sum to 0.949 rather than 1, and it varies from pixel to pixel with albedo, geometry and aerosol. Above all, it is not an approximation to anything: in the linear regime it is the exact response of the retrieval. A finer vertical grid does not make it go away.

4 · Where they meet: one row, two factors

A real observation operator for XCH₄ does both jobs. Interpolate each layer to the pixel, then collapse the layers. Row \(n\) of \(\mat{H}\) is therefore a Kronecker product of a vertical and a horizontal row vector, and stacking such rows is the face-splitting (row-wise Kronecker) product.

Concept

Separable prior, row-Kronecker operator

\[ \vect{H}_n = \tilde{\vect{a}}_n^\top \otimes \vect{w}_n^\top \] \[ \vect{H}_n (\mat{K}_z \otimes \mat{K}_s) \vect{H}_m^\top = (\tilde{\vect{a}}_n^\top \mat{K}_z \tilde{\vect{a}}_m)(\vect{w}_n^\top \mat{K}_s \vect{w}_m) \] \[ \op{Cov}(\vect{y}) = (\tilde{\mat{A}} \mat{K}_z \tilde{\mat{A}}^\top) \circ (\mat{W} \mat{K}_s \mat{W}^\top) + \mat{R} \]

The second line is the mixed-product rule \((\mat{P} \otimes \mat{Q})(\mat{S} \otimes \mat{T}) = \mat{P} \mat{S} \otimes \mat{Q} \mat{T}\) applied to a 1-row and 1-column factor. The column covariance is a Hadamard product: a vertical AK sandwich times a KISS-GP sandwich.

Where \(\vect{H}_n\)row \(n\) of the observation operator\(\mathbb{R}^{1 \times L G}\)— \(\circ\)elementwise (Hadamard) product\((N, N) \times (N, N)\)

This is the clean statement of your hunch: the averaging kernel is to height what KISS-GP's interpolation weights are to the horizontal. Both sit in \(\mat{H}\), on orthogonal axes, and with a separable prior they factor apart exactly.

Simple · one AK for all pixels

\(\tilde{\vect{a}}_n = \tilde{\vect{a}}\) for every \(n\). The vertical factor is one scalar:

\[ \op{Cov}(\vect{y}) = (\tilde{\vect{a}}^\top \mat{K}_z \tilde{\vect{a}}) \mat{W} \mat{K}_s \mat{W}^\top + \mat{R} \]

The column field is just a scaled 2-D KISS-GP. All the fast structure survives.

General · AK varies per pixel

\(\mat{C}_z\) is a dense \(N \times N\) matrix, generally rank \(\le L\):

\[ \op{Cov}(\vect{y}) = \mat{C}_z \circ \mat{C}_s + \mat{R} \]

MVMs still work: \((\mat{C}_z \circ \mat{C}_s) \vect{v} = \sum_r \op{diag}(\vect{u}_r) \mat{C}_s \op{diag}(\vect{u}_r) \vect{v}\) for a rank-\(r\) factor \(\mat{C}_z = \sum_r \vect{u}_r \vect{u}_r^\top\), i.e. at most \(L\) KISS-GP MVMs.

5 · What you were missing: the AK is a posterior smoother

So far the averaging kernel looks like just another row in \(\mat{H}\). Its name says more. Go one level down, to the L1B → L2 retrieval that produced it. There the observation is a radiance spectrum \(\vect{r}\) with \(M\) channels and the unknown is the profile \(\vect{x}\).

Model

The averaging kernel is the Jacobian of a MAP estimate

1Joint
\[ p(\vect{r}, \vect{x}) = p(\vect{x}) p(\vect{r} \mid \vect{x}) \]
2Factorisation

One pixel, one profile, so the joint is already the product of a prior and a likelihood; nothing further to factor.

3Objective
\[ \mathcal{J}(\vect{x}) = -\log p(\vect{r} \mid \vect{x}) - \log p(\vect{x}) \] \[ \hat{\vect{x}} = \op{argmin}_{\vect{x}} \mathcal{J}(\vect{x}) \]
4Assumptions
A1
Gaussian prior. \[ p(\vect{x}) = \mathcal{N}(\vect{x}; \vect{x}_a, \mat{S}_a) \] The retrieval's own prior; it differs from the inversion's \(\mat{K}_z\). Double counting them is a classic error.
A2
Linear forward model. \[ F(\vect{x}) \approx F(\vect{x}_a) + \mat{J} (\vect{x} - \vect{x}_a) \] Breaks for strong absorption or scattering; iterating Gauss–Newton gives the AK at the solution.
A3
Gaussian noise. \[ p(\vect{r} \mid \vect{x}) = \mathcal{N}(\vect{r}; F(\vect{x}), \mat{S}_\epsilon) \] Spectroscopic and model errors are not white; they enter \(\mat{S}_\epsilon\) only approximately.
5Resultuses A1–A3
\[ \hat{\mat{S}} = (\mat{J}^\top \mat{S}_\epsilon^{-1} \mat{J} + \mat{S}_a^{-1})^{-1} \] \[ \mat{G} = \hat{\mat{S}} \mat{J}^\top \mat{S}_\epsilon^{-1} \] \[ \hat{\vect{x}} = \vect{x}_a + \mat{A} (\vect{x} - \vect{x}_a) + \mat{G} \gvect{\epsilon} \] \[ \mat{A} = \mat{G} \mat{J} = \mat{I} - \hat{\mat{S}} \mat{S}_a^{-1} \]

The column AK of §3 is the pressure-weighted collapse of this matrix, \(\tilde{\vect{a}} = \mat{A}^\top \vect{h}\), so \(\vect{h}^\top \hat{\vect{x}} = \vect{h}^\top \vect{x}_a + \tilde{\vect{a}}^\top (\vect{x} - \vect{x}_a)\) plus noise. TROPOMI fits a scaled profile with other state elements, but its reported AK has this meaning.

Where \(\vect{r}\)radiance spectrum\(\mathbb{R}^{M}\)[L] \(\mat{S}_a\), \(\mat{S}_\epsilon\)retrieval prior and noise covariances\(\mathbb{R}^{L \times L}\), \(\mathbb{R}^{M \times M}\)ppb²[L]² \(\mat{J}\)Jacobian \(\partial F / \partial \vect{x}\) (Rodgers writes \(\mat{K}\))\(\mathbb{R}^{M \times L}\)[L] ppb⁻¹ \(\hat{\mat{S}}\)posterior covariance\(\mathbb{R}^{L \times L}\)ppb² \(\mat{G}\)gain\(\mathbb{R}^{L \times M}\)ppb [L]⁻¹ \(\mat{A}\)profile averaging kernel \(\partial \hat{\vect{x}} / \partial \vect{x}\)\(\mathbb{R}^{L \times L}\)—

Now take the case GP people know. Observe the state directly with white noise: \(\mat{J} = \mat{I}\), \(\mat{S}_a = \mat{K}\), \(\mat{S}_\epsilon = \sigma^2 \mat{I}\).

Simple · direct observation \[ \mat{A} = (\sigma^{-2} \mat{I} + \mat{K}^{-1})^{-1} \sigma^{-2} \] \[ \mat{A} = \mat{K} (\mat{K} + \sigma^2 \mat{I})^{-1} \]

This is the GP smoother (hat) matrix: the posterior mean at the data is \(\mat{A} \vect{y}\). Its rows are the "equivalent kernel" of spline and GP regression, and \(\op{tr}(\mat{A})\) is the effective degrees of freedom. Rodgers' DOFS is the same trace.

General · any Jacobian \[ \mat{A} = \mat{I} - \hat{\mat{S}} \mat{S}_a^{-1} \]

Read it as "how much of the prior variance the data removed". Where the instrument is blind, \(\hat{\mat{S}} \approx \mat{S}_a\) and the row of \(\mat{A}\) goes to zero: the estimate stays at \(\vect{x}_a\).

Running example

Take the 12-layer prior \(\mat{K}_z\) (20 ppb, 250 hPa) and observe each layer directly with \(\sigma = 15\) ppb. Then \(\op{tr}(\mat{A}) = 3.18\): twelve noisy numbers carry about three independent pieces of information. The surface row of \(\mat{A}\) is 0.40, 0.28, 0.14, 0.04, then near zero; it sums to 0.79. A local, smooth bump that sums to less than 1, with the remainder taken from the prior. Swap "layer" for "location" and you have a GP equivalent kernel.

Watch out

So "averaging kernel" and "kernel" in "kernel method" share a word for a reason, but not the one KISS-GP uses. The AK is a posterior smoother that already contains a prior and a noise model; \(\mat{W}\) is a prior-side approximation with no noise in it. If your inversion also puts a GP prior on \(\vect{x}\), the column you assimilate has been smoothed once by the retrieval and will be smoothed again by your GP.
